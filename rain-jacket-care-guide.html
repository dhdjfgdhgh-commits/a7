The Rain Jacket Care Guide

A rain jacket that has stopped working is usually just dirty or needs reproofing. A little care brings it back.

In this guide

  1. Why washing helps
  2. How to wash
  3. Reviving the DWR
  4. Drying and storage
  5. Small repairs
  6. Signs it’s time to retire

01Why washing helps

Dirt, sweat and body oils clog the pores of a membrane and stop the DWR finish working. Washing restores breathability and helps water bead again. Contrary to old advice, regular washing does not harm a good shell.

02How to wash

  1. Close all zips and Velcro, and empty the pockets.
  2. Use a technical wash, not normal detergent or fabric softener.
  3. Wash on a gentle, cool cycle and rinse twice.
  4. Clean the detergent drawer first so no residue gets on the jacket.

03Reviving the DWR

If water no longer beads after washing, tumble dry on low heat for 20 minutes (if the label allows) to reactivate the finish. If that does not work, apply a wash-in or spray-on reproofer and follow its instructions.

04Drying and storage

Hang the jacket to dry fully before storing it. Store it loosely on a hanger in a dry place rather than stuffed in a bag, which can crease and wear the membrane over time.

05Small repairs

  • Tears: use a repair patch made for technical fabrics.
  • Peeling seam tape: seam sealer can stop small leaks.
  • Sticky zips: clean with a soft brush; a zip lubricant helps.
  • Big damage: many outdoor repairers can replace zips and re-tape seams.

06Signs it’s time to retire

If the inside is delaminating, the membrane is cracked or leaks continue after washing, reproofing and seam sealing, the jacket has probably reached the end of its waterproof life. It can still serve as a windproof layer.
