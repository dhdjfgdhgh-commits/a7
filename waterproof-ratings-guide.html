The Waterproof Ratings Guide

Rain jacket labels are full of numbers. Here is what they measure, which ones matter for you and what they don’t tell you.

In this guide

  1. Hydrostatic head, in millimetres
  2. Breathability, in grams
  3. Membranes and coatings
  4. Seams, zips and hoods
  5. What the numbers don’t tell you
  6. Choosing for your weather

01Hydrostatic head, in millimetres

The waterproof rating is measured by placing a column of water on the fabric and raising it until water pushes through. A rating of 10,000 mm means the fabric held a 10-metre column before leaking.

RatingSuits
Under 5,000 mmLight showers, short exposure
5,000–10,000 mmEveryday rain, city use
10,000–20,000 mmHiking and regular outdoor use
Over 20,000 mmHeavy, prolonged rain and mountains

02Breathability, in grams

Breathability is usually given in grams of water vapour that pass through a square metre of fabric in 24 hours. Higher is more breathable. Around 10,000 g suits walking; 15,000–20,000 g or more suits fast hiking, running and cycling.

03Membranes and coatings

  • Membranes: a thin laminated layer with microscopic pores; generally the most breathable.
  • Coatings: a layer painted onto the inside of the fabric; usually lighter on the wallet and a little less breathable.
  • 2, 2.5 and 3-layer: how the membrane is protected. Three-layer shells are most durable; 2.5-layer are lightest.

04Seams, zips and hoods

A high fabric rating means little if water gets in elsewhere. Look for fully taped seams, water-resistant or covered zips, a hood with a stiff peak and adjusters, and cuffs that close around your wrists.

05What the numbers don’t tell you

Test conditions differ between makers, and real-world performance depends on fit, venting and how worn the DWR is. Treat ratings as a guide to compare jackets, not a guarantee. The decoders help you read them quickly.

06Choosing for your weather

For a city commute, a lighter 5,000–10,000 mm jacket with good venting is often more comfortable than a heavy mountain shell. For hill walking in changeable weather, go for 15,000 mm or more with a good hood.
